TabPFN v2: KV caching

WHY IT WORKS

Train rows never attend to test rows, so their states are fixed.

Measured: 300–800× faster repeat predictions (CPU).

Why TabPFN v2 can cache. In column attention, training rows attend only to training rows; test rows attend to training rows but nobody attends to test rows. So the training rows' states at every layer depend only on the training data. Fit runs the training rows through all 12 layers once and stores each layer's keys and values. Predict then runs only the test rows: at each layer they do their own row attention and read the cached keys and values for column attention. Per layer, the cost drops from recomputing everything, (n plus t) times d squared plus d times (n plus t) squared, to t times d squared plus t times n times d. The paper reports about 300 times faster prediction at 10 thousand rows and 10 features, and about 800 times at 100 features, on CPU. The trade-off is memory. A rough estimate, using 12 layers, 192-wide keys and values, features grouped in pairs and 16-bit floats: about 55 megabytes for a thousand rows and 10 features, 550 megabytes for ten thousand rows and 10 features, 4.7 gigabytes at 100 features, and about 23 gigabytes at the 500-feature limit, per ensemble member. These are my estimates from the model configuration, not published numbers. TabPFN-3 reduces this by sharing one key-value head across test queries, an eight-fold smaller cache.
